Treat the cylinder and heater as a single temperature node. The difference between supplied power and convection, radiation and additional heat loss determines how its temperature changes.

Constant air properties

Automatic mode: dry ideal air at 101325 Pa; Sutherland viscosity and conductivity, constant cₚ = 1006 J/(kg·K). This is an engineering approximation, restricted here to evaluation temperatures of 250–600 K. No online lookup. Manual mode keeps k, ν and Pr constant. Bead heat capacity is a separate property.

The table shows the integrated end-of-run temperature and heat transfer rates. Positive convection and radiation values represent heat leaving the cylinder. The dashed horizontal line solves the steady energy balance, including extra loss UA, independently of the run duration.

Model and assumptions

CsdTsdt=VI−Qconv(Ts)−Qrad(Ts)−UA(Ts−Ta)

Calibrate the effective heat capacity and additional heat loss coefficient UA against measured temperature histories. Convection uses the selected correlation. Morgan evaluates buoyancy using the cylinder–air film temperature.

Some material properties, dimensions and emissivities are example values. Check these inputs and assumptions before comparing with measurements. Temperatures in the radiation term are absolute temperatures (K).
